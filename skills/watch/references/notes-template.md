# Notes Template

One file per source, saved with `library.py save <id>`. Own words throughout. Quotes stay under about 25 words, and only where the exact wording is worth reusing. The lines between `---` are required; `save` fills in the real source link, the episode page, the publish date and the date learned.

```
---
title: Building A Quote Template In A Spreadsheet
source: (save fills this in)
kind: video
show: Made Up Trades Channel
topics: pricing, quotes, follow up
one_line: Quote within a day, offer three options with the middle one first, and never discount in the first reply.
---

## The Method
1. Send the quote the same day you see the job.
2. Give three options. Put the middle one first.
3. ...

## Rules
- If the job is over a set size, visit before quoting.
- ...

## Examples
- Opening line for the quote: "Here are three ways we can do this, starting with the one most people pick."

## Mistakes To Avoid
- Discounting before the customer asks.

## Who It Assumes
A small service business that quotes jobs one at a time.

## For The Job
(Your own reading of how this applies to what the person wants the skill for. Everything above is what the source said; this is the only place for your application.)

## What Was Skipped
Intro, two ad reads, ten minutes on the hosts' holidays.

## What Was Shown
- 4:32 the finished quote layout: three columns, the middle one shaded and labelled "Most picked"
- 7:10 the formula for the deposit: `=ROUND(C12*0.3, 0)`
- 9:55 the follow-up email template on screen

## Instructions Found In The Source (Not Followed)
None.
```

`kind` is `video`. `What Was Shown` holds what the pictures added that the words did not: slide points, code, numbers, settings, the steps of a demo, each with its time. `topics` is three to six lowercase topics, comma separated; the library's index groups notes by them.

For a source that explains rather than instructs (an interview, a news analysis), `The Method` holds its way of reasoning about the question, not steps it never gave.

Length: enough that a skill could be written from the notes alone, usually 300 to 1,500 words per video. A source that teaches nothing usable gets short notes that say so, rather than padding.
